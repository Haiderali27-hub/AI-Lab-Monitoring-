\section{Problem Statement / Limitations}

Running a computer-lab exam for forty students with one or two invigilators is harder than it looks. Supervisors cannot watch every screen at once. Students know this. Without a technical enforcement layer, policy compliance depends entirely on physical presence and trust \cite{mit_cbt}.

The problems in current lab-exam practice fall into three categories.

\textbf{No unified scheduling tool.} Exam dates, lab assignments, and seat maps live in spreadsheets, printed lists, or WhatsApp messages. Invigilators sometimes arrive to find a student sitting at the wrong machine, or no instructions posted in the room. Coordination breaks down because there is no single system of record.

\textbf{Weak live visibility.} Even experienced invigilators cannot simultaneously track what thirty students have open on their screens. Focus switches, unauthorized applications, and unusual inactivity go undetected. Without structured telemetry, these events are never logged and cannot be reviewed later.

\textbf{Poor evidence and traceability.} When a suspicious incident occurs during an exam, supervisors must reconstruct it from memory after the session ends. There is no automatic log of which applications were running, when a student went inactive, or what their screen showed at a given moment. This makes academic disputes very hard to resolve fairly.

These three problems compound each other. Fragmented scheduling causes confusion at the start. Weak monitoring misses incidents during the exam. Poor evidence makes resolution impossible afterward.

\textbf{Project-level limitations.} SmartExam is an FYP prototype, not a production enterprise system. The AI evaluation and face recognition modules represent solid proof-of-concept implementations. They are not production-trained classifiers. Large-scale institutional deployment — spanning multiple campuses, mixed operating systems, and enterprise SSO — falls outside the FYP boundary. These boundaries are deliberate and acknowledged, not oversights.
