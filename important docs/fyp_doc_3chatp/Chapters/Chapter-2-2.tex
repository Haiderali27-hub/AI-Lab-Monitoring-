Two rules apply across these requirements. Roles are kept apart, so only staff can read monitoring data \cite{rbac1}. No penalty is automatic: the \texttt{ViolationClassifier} labels events, and all AI marks are suggestions. The teacher makes the final decision.

\section{Requirement Gathering Techniques Used}
\label{sec:gathering}

We used structured interviews, paper prototypes and two brainstorming sessions \cite{elicit1, sommerville}.

\subsection{Interviews}
We asked teachers and lab coordinators how practical exams run today. Two needs came up most: watching workstations live, and keeping evidence that can settle disputes later.

\subsection{Prototyping}
We showed users paper prototypes of the login, dashboard, exam and submission screens \cite{sommerville}. Their feedback added a countdown timer on the exam screen, live alerts at the top of the teacher dashboard, and one question per screen.

\subsection{Brainstorming Session 1: Academic Workflow}
We mapped the life cycle of a lab exam and the manual steps that cost staff the most time. This set our first priorities: exam configuration, role management and seat assignment.

\subsection{Brainstorming Session 2: Monitoring and Reporting}
We discussed monitoring, proctoring and reports. Face recognition was left out of scope because of privacy concerns and the effort of reliable identity matching on lab webcams; the webcam only checks that it is not covered or frozen and that one face is visible. We also agreed on one PDF report combining attendance, violations and scores.
