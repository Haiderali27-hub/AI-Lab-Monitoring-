\section{Functional Requirements}
Table~\ref{tab:functionalrequirements} lists the functional requirements. FR$n$ belongs to module M$n$; module M11 has three (FR11.1--FR11.3). Each requirement states the limits that the tests in Chapter~\ref{chap:testing} check. Two rules apply across these requirements. Roles are kept apart, so only staff with access to an exam can read its monitoring data \cite{rbac1}. No penalty is automatic: the \texttt{ViolationClassifier} labels events, and all AI marks are suggestions that the teacher accepts or overrides; every override is audited.

FR1 & M1 Authentication and Device Binding & \textbf{Users log in; a student only from the bound workstation.} Log users in, allowing at most 10 login requests per minute from one IP address. Bind each student account to one workstation: the client sends its hardware ID (HWID: BIOS, system-disk and baseboard serials), the server stores only its SHA-256 hash, and a login from another PC is refused until an administrator resets the binding. Store passwords as salted PBKDF2 (Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2) hashes \cite{rfc8018} and issue JWTs \cite{rfc7519} that expire after 30 min, with single-use rotating refresh tokens. \\
FR2 & M2 Pre-Exam Verification and Practice Exam & \textbf{Students pass a readiness check and can take practice exams.} Before an exam, check the network (best API round trip at most 2 s), clock skew (at most 60 s from the server), screen capture, webcam, microphone, secure mode (one monitor, no Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) session, no virtual machine (VM), keyboard hook active) and free disk (at least 200 MB). When an exam requires readiness, a pass older than 30 min must be repeated. Offer practice exams with unlimited attempts, in Report lockdown mode and without the webcam; practice exams do not count towards results (they are auto-marked but never plagiarism-checked and are hidden from the student's results). \\
FR3 & M3 Student Exam Dashboard & \textbf{Students see their assigned exams, their status and their results.} In the desktop client, show the student's current assigned exam with its instructions, start countdown, readiness status and eligibility; Start stays disabled, with the reason shown, while the student is ineligible. List the practice exams. In the student web portal, show the student's exams, results (once the exam has ended), violation history, performance trend and notifications. Exams can be taken only in the desktop client. \\
FR4 & M4 Secure Exam Environment & \textbf{The client locks the workstation for the whole exam.} Run a fullscreen, topmost exam window; block Alt+Tab, the Windows key and Task Manager; guard the clipboard; report unauthorised processes (Report mode) or also close them (Enforce mode); detect newly mounted USB storage devices, extra monitors, remote sessions and VMs. \\
FR5 & M5 Live Monitoring Client Service & \textbf{The client reports activity, screenshots and webcam issues to the server.} Send a REST heartbeat every 10 s (focus, processes, idle time, camera status) and monitoring events in batches every 5 s. Take screenshots at random intervals around a per-exam mean (default 60 s, i.e. 30--90 s; 10--600 s allowed) and immediately on a violation. When the exam requires the webcam, analyse frames on the PC for seven issues (camera unavailable, covered or frozen; no face; multiple faces; head turned away, by a coarse head-pose check; phone in view), send one event per episode, and upload webcam snapshots at the exam's interval and when an issue is raised. \\
FR6 & M6 Submission and Backup & \textbf{Every answer is saved, backed up and submitted without loss.} Save each edited answer to the server 10 s after its first unsaved edit, and at once on a question change, focus loss or submit. Write every edit within about 1 s to an AES-256-GCM encrypted local backup, queue failed saves while offline and replay them in order, and after a crash resume with the server and backup answers merged. Return a SHA-256 receipt of the stored answers on submission. Enforce the deadline on the server (exam end plus the student's extra minutes and a 2-minute grace) and finalise overdue sessions as Timed out. \\
FR7 & M7 User and Role Management & \textbf{Administrators manage accounts, roles, departments and courses.} Let administrators create, edit, import (CSV), deactivate and delete accounts, force a logout, reset a device binding and assign the roles SuperAdmin, Admin, Teacher, Invigilator and Student; only a SuperAdmin may create or manage Admin and SuperAdmin accounts. Let administrators maintain the academic structure of departments and courses. \\
FR8 & M8 Exam Creation and Configuration & \textbf{Teachers create, configure and announce exams.} Let teachers and administrators create exams with multiple-choice (MCQ), coding and theory questions, test cases, an allowed-apps list, a lockdown mode (Off, Report or Enforce; default Report), the webcam requirement, and the screenshot and webcam intervals. Let them announce an exam and notify its students of the schedule and of their grades, in the app and by email (SMTP). \\
FR9 & M9 Eligibility and Access Control & \textbf{Teachers control who may sit an exam and where.} Set eligibility from imported attendance (default minimum 75\%) with audited manual overrides. Let administrators manage labs and workstations, and let the teacher assign invigilators and a workstation to each student. Allow the exam only from the assigned workstation (matched by machine name by default). \\
FR10 & M10 Live Proctoring Dashboard & \textbf{Invigilators watch every student live and act on issues.} Show a live tile per student over SignalR: Stable (Normal), Warning, Suspicious or Critical (Violation) from the event classes, Disconnected after 30 s of silence, and Submitted, Force submitted or Timed out at the end. Let staff (invigilators only on their assigned exams) send warnings, extend one student's time (1--180 min per grant, at most 600 min in total), unlock the PC (5 min from the drawer; the server allows 1--30 min) and relock it, record incidents (Low to Critical), force a submission, and view each student's timeline, screenshots and webcam snapshots. Commands reach the client in its next heartbeat reply. \\
FR11.1 & M11 AI-Assisted Evaluation & \textbf{The server grades C and C++ code against test cases.} Compile C and C++ coding answers and run them against weighted test cases (default limit 2 s per case) in an isolated Docker \texttt{gcc:13} container when Docker is available; otherwise use a local MinGW g++ runner, which is not isolated, and with no runner leave the answer for teacher review. \\
FR11.2 & M11 AI-Assisted Evaluation & \textbf{The server marks MCQ answers and scores theory answers.} Mark MCQ answers as full or zero. Score theory answers against the model answer with offline TF-IDF similarity \cite{salton1988} (0.7 cosine similarity plus 0.3 key-term coverage) and a confidence value. Large language model (LLM) grading is optional and off by default. \\
FR11.3 & M11 AI-Assisted Evaluation & \textbf{Teachers see code similarity between submissions and flagged pairs.} Compare the submissions of each non-practice exam pairwise by k-gram winnowing \cite{winnowing} (k = 5, window 4), show a similarity matrix, and flag pairs at or above the exam's threshold (default 70\%) for the teacher. \\
FR12 & M12 Logs, Analytics and Audit Trail & \textbf{Staff get a verifiable audit log, analytics, reports and exports.} Keep a tamper-evident audit log of security-relevant and staff actions, in which each row is chained to the previous one by a SHA-256 hash, and let administrators verify the chain. Produce exam analytics, an exam summary PDF, separate attendance, violations and scores PDFs, an Excel workbook and an evidence ZIP with a SHA-256 manifest; each export is audited with its file hash. \\
\end{longtable}}

NFR1 & Security & Passwords are stored as PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes with a 32-byte salt and 600,000 iterations \cite{owasp_pwd}; the iteration count is stored with each hash, so older hashes are upgraded at the next login \cite{nist80063b}. Access tokens expire after 30 min; refresh tokens are single-use and rotate, and reusing one revokes its whole family. Login allows 10 and refresh 30 requests per minute per IP address, then returns HTTP 429. Every endpoint except the two login endpoints, token refresh and the health check requires a valid JWT and a permitted role. In a lab deployment all traffic shall use HTTPS (NFR8). \\
NFR2 & Reliability & A network outage of any length shall not lose an answer: every edit reaches the encrypted local backup within about 1 s, failed saves and monitoring events are queued and sent in order when the server is reachable again, and a restarted client resumes the session. An automatic submit at time-out retries every 10 s until the server answers. \\
NFR3 & Usability (interaction capability) & A student shall be able to log in, pass the readiness check and start an exam using only the on-screen instructions. Every refusal (failed check, ineligibility, wrong workstation, failed login) shows its reason, and final actions (submitting with unanswered questions, force-submitting) ask for confirmation. \\
NFR4 & Performance (performance efficiency) & With 40 desktop clients (the upper end of the 30--40 lab machines in Section~\ref{sec:productpurpose}) sending heartbeats every 10 s, login, exam start, answer saves and heartbeats shall complete within 2 s at the 95th percentile. The readiness check fails a PC whose best API round trip exceeds 2 s. \\
NFR5 & Maintainability & One ASP.NET Core 9 API organised by module. Every module M1--M12 has automated tests (xUnit for the API and client logic, Playwright for the web panel) that run headless, so a full regression needs no lab PC. \\
NFR6 & Data Integrity (security: integrity) & The server enforces the deadline, so a changed client clock cannot extend an exam. A save with an older revision than the stored answer is refused. Each submission returns a SHA-256 receipt of the stored answers, and verifying the hash-chained audit log reveals a changed row or a gap in the sequence. \\
\end{longtable}}

\section{Requirement Gathering Techniques Used}
\label{sec:gathering}

We used structured interviews, paper prototypes, two brainstorming sessions and an analysis of existing systems \cite{elicit1, sommerville}. Table~\ref{tab:gathering} links what each technique showed to the requirements it produced.

\subsection{Interviews}
We asked teachers and lab coordinators how practical exams run today. Two needs came up most: watching workstations live, and keeping evidence that can settle disputes later.

\subsection{Prototyping}
We showed users paper prototypes of the login, dashboard, exam and submission screens \cite{sommerville}. Their feedback added a countdown timer on the exam screen, live alerts at the top of the teacher dashboard, and one question per screen.

\subsection{Brainstorming Session 1: Academic Workflow}
We mapped the life cycle of a lab exam and the manual steps that cost staff the most time. This set the order in which we built the foundation: role management, exam configuration and seat assignment (Sprints 2 and 3).

\subsection{Brainstorming Session 2: Monitoring and Reporting}
We discussed monitoring, proctoring and reports. Face recognition was left out of scope because of privacy concerns and the effort of reliable identity matching on lab webcams, so the webcam checks look only at the camera state and the faces in view; the coarse head-pose and phone checks were added later. We also agreed that every exam needs reports on attendance, violations and scores; these became separate PDF exports beside an exam summary PDF.

\subsection{Analysis of Existing Systems}
Section~\ref{sec:existingsystems} reviews ten existing tools, and Table~\ref{tab:comparison} compares them feature by feature. No tool binds a student to a lab workstation or runs code against test cases, and only MOSS checks code for plagiarism. Some tools offer lockdown or live monitoring, but none combines them with these features for an exam in a physical lab. These gaps set FR1, FR4, FR9, FR10, FR11.1 and FR11.3.
