\section{Problem Statement / Limitations}

As much as convenient a computer lab can be, administering a live computer lab exam to around 40 students using one or two supervisors is more challenging than it appears, not least because supervisors have limitations in physically overseeing the entire lab. Students are aware of such limitation. Thus, without technological enforcement, policy violation is at best a matters of opportunity \cite{mit_cbt}.

We found the current practice of running computer lab exams problematic due to the following three categories of issues.

\textbf{Disparate schedule management.} Numerous records exist regarding schedule and location of exams, including examination date, lab assigned, student's seating plan, to spreadsheets, printed documents or even group chats. As a result, supervisors may face challenges such as a student occupying the wrong machine, or lack of scheduled instruction in the exam room, hindering the coordination and creating confusion. Without a unified system of record, proper coordination remains elusive.

\textbf{Weak live visibility.} Supervisors may lack adequate tools to monitor the activity of multiple students at once, especially if 30+ students are present in a room. Unauthorized software, shifts in focus, and inactivity may go unnoticed by supervisors without real-time telemetry for later analysis \cite{elicit1}.

\textbf{Inadequate evidential traceability.} When an incident occurs, supervisors may find themselves in a difficult position when trying to recall event details from their memory, with the challenge of gathering detailed evidence, including application launches, student's session time, and their screen activities \cite{rbac1, elicit1}. Such a situation further complicates any academic dispute resolution process.

The three issues discussed are interwoven. Problems with schedule planning create confusion and problems on examination morning. Lack of real-time system monitoring miss such issues when they happen. And a poorly maintained trace makes it impossible to resolve disputes afterward.

\textbf{System boundaries.} The scope of the FYP is for the development of a prototype; therefore, features like advanced AI evaluations, student face recognition, or full-blown enterprise solutions for multiple campus institutions with mixed operating systems and enterprise single sign-on (SSO) integration fall outside the scope of this project and are considered as future enhancements, beyond the capabilities of a two-semester project.
